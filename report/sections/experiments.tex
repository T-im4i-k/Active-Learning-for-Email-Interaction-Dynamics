\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

For each variant we grid-search its hyperparameters and select the
configuration maximizing validation-set Recall-AUC,
$\mathrm{AUC} = \int_0^1 \mathrm{Recall}(\tau)\,d\tau$, where
$\mathrm{Recall}(\tau)$ is recall at sending fraction $\tau$. We report
test-set Recall@5/15/25/35\% and Recall-AUC, mean $\pm$ std over repeated
simulations. Before testing modifications we reproduced the original model
on our pipeline; at the operating points reported by
\citet{zid2025active} (25/50/75\%) our reproduction matches
the published point estimates exactly ($0.923$, $0.975$, $0.989$), with
larger variance attributable to fewer simulation runs.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Gains are largest at low sending fractions and shrink as $\tau$ grows,
since baseline recall already exceeds 0.92 by $\tau=25\%$. The
\textbf{variance-based $p_j$} (0.479 at 5\%), \textbf{recency-weighted
$\phi_j$} (0.447), and \textbf{factored $s_j(t)$} (0.425) give the
largest low-fraction gains and some of the highest AUC (0.905, 0.903, 0.900 vs.\
0.894 baseline). The \textbf{forward-pass $f_j(t)$} shows no gain at 5\%
but a consistent uplift from 15\% onward, suggesting nonlinear
aggregation only helps once significant number of recipients have opened. The
$\alpha$-scheduling variants are largely neutral, and the
\textbf{Binary-to-TTO autoencoder} underperforms across all quantiles.
\textbf{TTO cutoff} gives a small, consistent uplift at no interpretive
cost, while the \textbf{deep autoencoder} improves recall modestly but
with markedly higher variance, suggesting the added capacity is not yet
well-regularized. All results test one modification at a time;
combinations are untested.
