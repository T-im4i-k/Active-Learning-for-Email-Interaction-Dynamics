\subsection{Incorporating Time-to-Open (TTO)}
\label{sec:tto}

The SAE is trained purely on binary opens, discarding \emph{how fast} a
recipient opened --- signal that matters given the short operational
window ($T/b$, $T$). Let $\Delta_{i,j}\in\R_{\ge0}\cup\{+\infty\}$ be the
observed time-to-open (with $\Delta_{i,j}=+\infty$ for non-openers, so
$X_{i,j}=\mathbbm{1}[\Delta_{i,j}<\infty]$), and define a decayed open
label
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Y-decay}
Y_{i,j} = 2^{-\Delta_{i,j}/h_\delta}, \qquad 2^{-\infty} := 0,
\end{equation}
with TTO half-life $h_\delta$; as $h_\delta\to\infty$, $Y_{i,j}\to
X_{i,j}$ for every opener.

\paragraph{Binary-to-TTO autoencoder.}
We keep the SAE's binary input but replace the reconstruction target:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:binary-to-tto}
\min_{E,D}\ \ell\big(Y,\ \sigma(XB_{E,D})\big).
\end{equation}
Since the input space is unchanged, the forward-pass definitions of $p_j$
and $f_j(t)$ (Eqs.~\eqref{eq:f-forward}--\eqref{eq:p-variance}) carry over
mechanically; only their interpretation shifts --- $\Sigma_{j,i}$ now
blends \emph{whether} $i$ opens with \emph{how fast}.

\paragraph{TTO cutoff.}
Training rows of $X$ reflect the \emph{eventual} open pattern, while
active-learning queries $x(t)$ are necessarily censored at $t < T$. We
instead censor the training input itself, fixing a cutoff $\delta_c$ and
defining $C_{i,j} = \mathbbm{1}[\Delta_{i,j}\le\delta_c]$, then training
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tto-cutoff}
\min_{E,D}\ \ell\big(X,\ \sigma(CB_{E,D})\big).
\end{equation}
$C$ retains only opens fast enough to plausibly be observed within an
active operational window and treats slower opens as non-opens during
training --- matching the censoring recipients are actually subject to
during active learning. As $\delta_c\to\infty$, $C\to X$, recovering the
original model.
