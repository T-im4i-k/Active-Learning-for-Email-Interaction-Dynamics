\subsection{Negative Feedback via a Second SAE}
\label{sec:negative-feedback}

The model of Section~\ref{sec:recap} learns from opens alone: a recipient who
was contacted but did not open is encoded exactly as one who was never
contacted, although an ignored email is evidence against opening for similar
recipients. We recover this signal through the send matrix
$S\in\{0,1\}^{n\times m}$, where $S_{i,j}=1$ if template $i$ was sent to
recipient $j$ (so $X_{i,j}\le S_{i,j}$).

\paragraph{Negative channel.}
We write $X^{+}=X$ and define the sent-but-unopened matrix
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:x-neg}
X^{-}_{i,j} = S_{i,j}\,\big(1-X_{i,j}\big)\in\{0,1\}.
\end{equation}
We train two SAEs, $(E^{+},D^{+})$ on $X^{+}$ and $(E^{-},D^{-})$ on
$X^{-}$, each with the objective of Section~\ref{sec:recap} and the same
bottleneck size $d$, and write $B^{\pm}=B_{E^{\pm},D^{\pm}}$. At time $t$ the
state of template $n{+}1$ splits accordingly: $x^{+}(t)=x(t)$ marks
recipients who have opened, and $x^{-}(t)\in\{0,1\}^m$ marks recipients who
were sent the template in $[0,t)$ and have not opened it.

\paragraph{Logit blending.}
Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:z-pm}
z^{+}_j(t) = x^{+}(t)^\top B^{+}e_j,
\qquad
z^{-}_j(t) = -\,x^{-}(t)^\top B^{-}e_j
\end{equation}
be the positive and negative logits. The minus sign encodes the direction
of the evidence: a large $B^{-}_{i,j}$ means that $i$ failing to open makes
$j$ failing more likely, which must \emph{lower} $f_j(t)$. We blend the
channels in logit space, applying the logit scaling $\gamma$ of
Section~\ref{sec:forward-f} to the result:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:f-neg}
f_j(t) = \sigma\Big(\gamma\big[\lambda z^{+}_j(t) + (1-\lambda)z^{-}_j(t)\big]\Big),
\qquad \lambda\in[0,1].
\end{equation}
Since the blend acts before $\sigma$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:f-neg} extends the
forward-pass definition of Section~\ref{sec:forward-f}: for $\lambda=1$ it
reduces to Eq.~\eqref{eq:f-forward}, and with no evidence it returns the
neutral prior $\tfrac12$ of the original SAE.

The weight $\lambda$ is needed because, with open rates around $9\%$,
$X^{-}$ is roughly ten times denser than $X^{+}$, so an unweighted
$z^{-}_j(t)$ would accumulate far more terms and dominate; moreover, a
missing open is weaker evidence than an open, as the recipient may simply not
have read the email yet. Both SAEs are trained once offline, so the
active-learning phase remains free of retraining, and $f_j(t)$ from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:f-neg} can replace $f_j(t)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:score} or in
$\pi_j(t)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pi-u} without further changes.
