\subsection{Recency-Weighted Historic Rate $\phi_j$}
\label{sec:recency-phi}

The original $\phi_j$ weights every past template equally, ignoring drift
in recipient behavior over time --- a limitation well studied in
time-aware collaborative filtering \citep{ding2005time,koren2009collaborative}.
Assuming templates
$X_{1:},\dots,X_{n:}$ were sent at uniform intervals, we assign template
$i$ an exponential weight, following the time-weighting scheme of
\citet{ding2005time},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omega}
\omega_i = 2^{-d_i/h}, \qquad d_i = \frac{n-i}{n},
\end{equation}
with half-life $h>0$, and redefine
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phi-weighted}
\phi_j = \frac{\omega^\top X_{:j}}{\sum_i \omega_i}.
\end{equation}
Normalizing by $\sum_i\omega_i$ keeps $\phi_j\in[0,1]$. As $h\to\infty$
every $\omega_i\to1$, recovering the original unweighted average as a
special case.

Figure~\ref{fig:phi-p-s}a illustrates the drift that this weighting is meant
to capture. For two selected recipients from each of four behavioral patterns
found in the training data, it compares the unweighted $\phi_j$ (the right end
of each curve) with the recency-weighted $\phi_j$ for $h=0.1$ (the marker at
the right edge). \emph{Late bloomers} open nothing in the first 100 templates
and about half of the last 29; the plain average ($0.14$ and $0.11$) underrates
them, whereas the weighted rate ($0.33$ and $0.37$) reflects their current
activity. \emph{Dropouts} are the mirror case: they open roughly nine in ten of
the first 100 templates but at most one of the last 29, so the plain average
($0.68$ and $0.65$) keeps ranking them highly, while the weighted rate ($0.18$
and $0.11$) shows that they have gone quiet. The two \emph{intermittent}
patterns show that a single average also blends regimes of very different
activity. Recipients who opened, paused and then returned
(on$\to$off$\to$on) have a plain average of only $0.27$ and $0.30$ because of
the pause, although they opened $66\%$ and $59\%$ of the last 29 templates;
their weighted rate is $0.52$ and $0.53$. Recipients who were active mainly
in the middle of the history (off$\to$on$\to$off) still score $0.28$ and $0.21$
although they have stopped opening, whereas their weighted rate is $0.11$ and
$0.09$. Such shifts are not rare: in the training data, weighting with
$h=0.1$ changes $\phi_j$ by more than $0.1$ for $10.4\%$ of recipients and by
more than $0.05$ for $19.4\%$.
